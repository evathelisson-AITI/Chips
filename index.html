Why this observatory exists

A new collateral class is forming: loans secured by GPUs, leased to AI companies, syndicated, securitized, and resold to insurers and pension funds. Its defining feature has no precedent: the collateral's depreciation clock is controlled by the vendor that sells the chips and invests in the financing vehicles. No systematic public dataset existed. This observatory builds it, openly, before the asset class is first tested.

The four components

1 · Deal Tracker. Hand-collected terms of chip-backed loans, leases, bonds and securitizations, each entry carrying its source and a verification flag.

2 · GPU Collateral Price Index. Resale and rental-rate series for major accelerator generations: the observable value of the collateral behind the loans.

3 · Announcement-Event Calendar. Dated vendor architecture events, past and announced. These scheduled, public events are what reprice the entire collateral base, and the backbone of event-study research.

4 · Holdings Map. Who ultimately holds the risk, mapped from insurance statutory filings and fund disclosures. In active collection.

Companion research: Silicon as Collateral: Vendor-Supported Chip-Backed Debt and the Financing of the AI Buildout (Thelisson, working paper, 2026).

Reading the two charts together: early securitizations reportedly assumed ~50% residual value after three years. The observed resale path fell to ~27% in that window, while the rental rates underpinning lease payments fell by 70–80% from peak before partially recovering.

Methodology & Contribute

Method

Entries are compiled exclusively from public sources: issuer press releases, offering materials, regulatory filings, published market trackers, and the financial press. Every entry carries its source. Primary-source confirmation earns a VERIFIED flag; press-only items remain REPORTED. Only a human curator assigns these flags. Nothing on this page is investment advice, and inclusion of a transaction implies no judgment about any named party. Corrections are welcome and acknowledged.

How the data are updated

The deal registry is curated by hand, entry by entry: verification is the product. To find candidates, an automated source watch scans SEC filings, press-release wires and the financial press twice a week and sends what it finds to the curator for review. It does not publish anything to the registry. Leads it collected earlier, before this rule was adopted, are listed below the deal table as PENDING REVIEW. They are excluded from every figure until the curator verifies or removes them. Technically, the page loads its data at every visit from a separate file (observatory-data.json) published alongside it, so updates appear to all visitors the moment the file is replaced, and the "Data as of" date in the header shows the release you are viewing. You can also press "Check for updates" at any time.

Openness

The database is released under CC BY 4.0. Quarterly snapshots will be archived with a citable reference. Researchers may reuse the data with attribution to the AI Transparency Institute.
